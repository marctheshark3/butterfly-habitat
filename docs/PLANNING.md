# Planning notes

For the next session. Not a print approval. Camera pod stays off.

## What is true now

The cube is a stack of plates. The front butts the floor, the two sides, and the roof. It does not snap, and nothing screws it on. The four holes in the front corners exit into empty air. The floor corner holes miss the walls the same way.

The door drops into three lips and lifts out the open top. Its screws match the door clamp only. They pinch a bought sheet. They do not enter the front.

The side walls are 188 mm. They stop at the inside face of the back. The earlier overlap, about 7040 mm3, is gone. The back stays a full 200 mm frame. The side clamp is its own part, `habitat-clamp-side`, printed twice. Project gate passed at revision 0.3.0.

The roof lifts off. That is the hatch. It is also not screwed to the cube.

## Still open: how the cube stays together

Do not pretend the current holes are a joint. The next session picks one fastener and cuts only that.

Options, in the order I would try them:

1. Screws through the front border into the front edge of each side, and the same at the back. The sides are 6 mm thick, so this is an end-grain screw into a short edge. It needs a real hole in that edge, not the mesh-clamp holes that already exist.
2. A tab on the front that drops into a slot in the floor, plus the roof sitting on it so it cannot lift. Still not a snap. Still needs a wind check.
3. Leave it a butt and glue it. Fine for a one-off. Bad if the girls need to take it apart.

The roof has the same problem, and it has to stay removable. A fastener there has to hold in the wind and still lift off with a chrysalis attached to the mesh.

## Pupation

"Patch" here means pupate. A monarch ready to pupate crawls up, lays a silk pad, hangs in a J from its rear prolegs, then hooks the cremaster into that pad as the skin sheds. In mesh pop-up cages they do this on the roof mesh. Do not move them once the pad is started. If the chrysalis is on the lid, the lid moves with it.

Sources: Monarch Butterfly Garden on mesh-roof pupation, Tyrant Farms on the J-hang and the mesh ceiling, Monarch Joint Venture on the silk pad and cremaster.

The roof sheet in the inspector is that surface. It is a 0.3 mm stand-in, not a measured cloth. Organza and no-see-um may be slicker than the mesh in those cage photos. That is the open question, not a new printed part.

Do not design a stick holder. A stick can be dropped in later if the sheet will not hold silk. A rough strip on the roof underside is the other low-parts option, and only if a real sheet fails. Not this session.

## Letting them out

The hatch is the whole roof, not a door in the roof. Lift the roof, the sheet, and the roof clamp as one stack. A chrysalis on that sheet comes with the roof. That is how you move one. It is also how you can tear a silk pad if you rush it.

Daily access is the front door. Release is after the adult has emerged and dried, then the roof comes off or the door opens. Do not pull a wet butterfly. Do not add a second hatch until the roof fastener is decided. The exploded view already lifts that stack so the motion is visible.
